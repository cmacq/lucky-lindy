## The campaign in a minute

The project follows the story from Lindbergh’s early flying and airmail work through the design of the *Spirit of St. Louis* and the May 20–21, 1927 flight. The centennial campaign opens on **November 3, 2026**, marking the date of the Covell, Illinois airmail parachute jump described in the project’s research brief.

The 1927 flight was the **first solo, nonstop flight from New York to Paris**—not the first transatlantic flight overall. The core story ends with the landing at Le Bourget on May 21.

## How each chapter works

Each weekly release uses a small, repeatable chapter kit: **one sourced historical claim, one short story, one visual or map, one audience question, and one clear next step.** Larger milestones can also receive a longer audio or video treatment and a local event.

## Editorial guardrails

- **Label dramatizations.** A period-style announcer or reenactment must be identified as a modern dramatization. Never imitate a real breaking-news report in a way that could be mistaken for current news.
- **Keep the scope accurate.** Describe the 1927 achievement as the first solo, nonstop New York–Paris flight.
- **Source claims and quotes.** The release calendar is a working plan; researchers should verify dates and details before scripts are recorded.
- **Protect young contributors.** Obtain school/guardian permission before publishing identifiable student media. Use opt-in contact lists.
- **Respect rights and access.** Clear footage, photographs, music, and archival material; provide captions, transcripts, and alt text.

## Starting research

The working brief points to the Minnesota Historical Society, National Postal Museum, National Air and Space Museum, Library of Congress, and Lindbergh’s published timeline and flight log. The history lead should maintain a source ledger with claim, source, confidence, approved wording, and rights status before any item is marked ready to publish.
