## Purpose

Create 60–120 second audio chapters that make the history vivid while keeping fact, period language, and modern reconstruction distinct. The voice should feel curious and human, not like an invented historical recording.

## Launch script · 90 seconds

**Working title:** *Before Paris, there was the mail route*  
**Format:** present-day host with clearly labeled sound reconstruction  
**Status:** draft for history review; verify all details and pronunciation before recording.

**[SFX: Present-day room tone. A brief, clearly designed radio static transition.]**

**HOST:** This is a modern dramatization—not a recording from 1926.

A century ago, an airmail pilot came down by parachute in a field near Covell, Illinois. His name was Charles Lindbergh. Some period accounts called him “Slim.” He was carrying the mail, and the flight did not go as planned.

That moment is a long way from the image most people remember: one pilot, one airplane, and the Atlantic. But the Paris flight began with years of flying, hard decisions, experiments, backers, mechanics, and routes like the one that brought Lindbergh over Illinois.

On May 20, 1927, Lindbergh left New York in the *Spirit of St. Louis*. The next day he landed in Paris, completing the first solo, nonstop flight from New York to Paris.

From this week through the centennial, we’ll follow the story one milestone at a time: the early flying, the mail route, the airplane, and the crossing. Follow the timeline and join us for the next chapter.

**[SFX: Static resolves into a simple present-day musical tag. Do not use uncleared period music.]**

## Reusable chapter template

1. **Open (5–10 sec):** a concrete sound, question, place, or decision.
2. **Set the scene (15–25 sec):** date and context, with one source-backed claim.
3. **Tell the moment (30–60 sec):** what happened and why it mattered.
4. **Make the distinction (as needed):** identify reenactment, uncertain detail, or competing accounts.
5. **Invite (5–10 sec):** question for listeners and one action: follow, attend, or explore the source notes.

## Production notes

- For May 20–21 chapters, adapt the [telegram dispatches]({{ '/telegrams/' | relative_url }}) as a ground-based wire-desk reading. Introduce them as modern reconstructions, use a brief telegraph sound cue, and direct listeners to the EAA129 chapter event page in the episode description.

- Put “modern dramatization” in the spoken intro when period-style sound or dialogue is used, and in the episode description.
- Do not invent a Lindbergh radio transmission. The project brief says he did not transmit radio reports from the airplane.
- Do not invent quotations. Read exact quotations only after verifying their source and rights.
- Use sound effects as atmosphere, not as evidence. Keep the voice clear and intelligible.
- Deliver a mastered file, transcript, source notes, and a short captioned social cut for each episode.
